# Credit monetization — research memo

## 1. What this is

Skipper has a credit ledger (`credit_entries`), a wall at `POST /drives`, and nothing on sale. 1.1 adds a conversational front door that spends real money per anonymous request. This memo asks whether the ledger is the right instrument, what the category actually does in 2026, what Apple currently permits, and what would have to be true before a single dollar could change hands. It recommends one shape and defends it. It is **not** a build order, and it does not authorize any spend. The single irreversible item in here is the size of the free grant — see §7 and §8 — because a grant's amount is frozen into the ledger row at write time (`apps/api/src/credits.ts`), so it can never be changed for a user who already has one.

---

## 2. The five things that would change Skipper's mind

**1. A vendor ran Skipper's exact experiment — metering a conversational agent — and killed it in public, naming the harm.** Windsurf retired credits on 2026-03-19: its credit model *"led users to be scared of asking quick questions, knowing they'd consume the same credits as a lengthy, complex task"* and made them *"cram multiple requests into single prompts rather than working interactively with the agent, ultimately degrading the quality of their Windsurf experience."* Replaced with plain daily/weekly quotas. VERIFIED, primary — https://devin.ai/blog/windsurf-pricing-plans/. For a product whose bet is that the persona *is* the product, a rider who batches their intent into one careful message to conserve credits has already lost the thing being sold.

**2. But "nobody meters a planning conversation" is false, and the memo should not lean on it.** Lovable charges **1 credit per Plan-Mode message** while a simple build costs **0.50** — a planning turn that produces nothing costs 2× a build that produces code (VERIFIED, https://lovable.dev/pricing, fetched 2026-07-31). Descript charges for chatting with Underlord even when no tool runs, and double-charges the "agent brain" plus the tool (VERIFIED, https://help.descript.com/hc/en-us/articles/27841674958221-). Replit: *"All Agent interactions are billable,"* including text-only guidance (VERIFIED, https://docs.replit.com/billing/ai-billing). The correct framing is: **the precedent exists in three products; one abolished it citing behavioural harm, one publicly committed to un-bundling dialogue from output and a year later still hasn't shipped it, and one still runs it.** The argument against metering the planner is evidentiary, not "unprecedented."

**3. The revealed price of a driving audio tour is $14.99–$19.99, not $2.99.** Shaka Guide Lake Tahoe **$16.99**, Yosemite $16.99, Yellowstone $18.99; GuideAlong Yellowstone **$19.99**; Action Tour Guide **$16.99/car**. Walking tours are the ones that live at $4.99–$9.99. All VERIFIED from vendor pages, fetched 2026-07-31 (https://www.shakaguide.com/tours, https://guidealong.com/, https://actiontourguide.com/). Every one of them markets **"never expires, use it on your next vacation"** as the headline benefit. Skipper's "lifetime, no expiry, never refunded on delete" is category-normal on both counts. The mismatch with the category is not the refund rule — it is the *unit*.

**4. `FREE_DRIVE_CAP` at today's value (100 — the value's home is `apps/api/src/credits.ts` + env; quoted here as a dated snapshot) is not a free tier. It is permanent free access, and it is a one-way door per user.** A rider takes 2–6 road trips a year. 100 lifetime drives is **17 to 50 years**. The ledger's own comment says why this matters: *"a grant's amount is frozen when it's written (the ledger is immutable), so raising this only affects users not yet granted."* Lowering it is equally one-way. **If Skipper ever sells a credit pack, the entire launch cohort — the people most likely to love it — will never buy one.** a16z's benchmark for a too-generous free plan is <5% cohorted annual conversion (VERIFIED, https://a16z.com/how-to-optimize-your-free-tier-freemium/); Skipper's would be ~0% by construction, for the life of those accounts.

**5. Apple's rule is narrower than three of the eight reports assumed, and the narrow reading hands Skipper its only credit-side cost lever.** Verbatim, fetched 2026-07-31: *"Any credits or in-game currencies **purchased via in-app purchase** may not expire…"* (VERIFIED, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/, Guideline 3.1.1; unchanged by the 2026-06-08 revision, which touched only the Introduction, 1.2, 4.3(a), 4.3(b), 4.5.3 — https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=a233fmpw). Purchased credits can never expire. **A free grant is not purchased and is outside 3.1.1's text.** The `expires_at` column already exists on `credit_entries`, reserved. Expiring or shrinking the *free* grant is legally and store-rule clean; expiring a *bought* one is not.

## 3. The landscape — how credit systems actually work in 2026

### 3a. The mechanics that actually differ

**Unit denomination.** Nobody in consumer creative AI denominates the ledger in the *user's artifact*. Not one vendor. The split is between ledgers that are cost-denominated but marketed in artifacts, and ledgers that are cost-denominated all the way to the user's face.

| Ledger denominated in | Vendors | Dynamic range of one credit |
|---|---|---|
| Raw compute time | Midjourney (GPU minutes), Leonardo ("Fast Tokens") | ~33× (0.8 min SD image → 26 min HD video batch) |
| Output duration | Runway (12 cr/sec), Luma (4–959 cr/sec), HeyGen (3–90 cr/min) | Luma **240×**, HeyGen ~30× |
| Input volume | ElevenLabs (1 credit ≈ 1 character), Descript (media minutes) | ~1×, flat and countable |
| Abstract per-generation | Suno, Udio, Ideogram, Kling, Magnific | Ideogram 0.5–6, ~12× |

The tell for cost-denomination is a wide range plus a disclaimer. Runway prints it: *"these are rates and do not indicate the total cost of a single generation"* (VERIFIED, https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/15124877443219-). Luma's 240× spread means a 10,000-credit plan buys either 2,500 seconds of Ray3.14 draft or 10 seconds of Seedance 4K — the number tells the user nothing.

Three behaviours confirm users don't understand cost units:
- **Every vendor restates the balance in artifacts at the point of sale.** Suno markets 50 credits as "10 songs/day" and *never states credits-per-song anywhere on the page* (VERIFIED, https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2410049) — third-party guides consequently disagree on whether a song costs 5 or 10. HeyGen's Free plan **abandons credits entirely**: *"1–3 free videos, depending on your region."* Udio prints a hard artifact cap ("3 full-length song generations per day") next to the credit line.
- **Two vendors independently invented the same fix — a price tag on the button.** HeyGen: *"HeyGen will show the credit cost in-product before you generate."* ElevenLabs: *"the number of credits that will be used for the generation will be displayed."* If the unit were legible you wouldn't need this.
- **Cost-denomination admits defeat on agentic work.** Descript: *"There's no fixed price per action, but your Usage tab shows exactly what each run cost"* — guarded by a **30-credit-per-file ceiling** on Studio Sound / Remove Filler Words plus a post-hoc audit log. That is the closest structural analogue in the whole corpus to `POST /drives/plan`: when the action is a conversation, per-action pricing stops existing and the only workable guards are **a cap and a log**.

**Refill vs top-up vs expiry.** The category has converged on a two-clock design: subscription credits expire at the cycle; purchased credits live much longer or forever. Kling writes it into the contract — subscription credits valid **1 month**, purchased credits valid **2 years**, promo credits per-campaign, consumed **FIFO by shortest remaining validity** (VERIFIED, https://klingai.com/global/docs/payment-policy, effective 2026-04-21). Kling also defines credits as non-money: *"the Credit is not a token, virtual currency or prepaid voucher, and does not have a monetary value."* Rollover caps cluster at 3× monthly (ElevenLabs "two months' worth"; Leonardo names it "Token Bank" at exactly 3×).

**Failure-refunds.** The line the category settled on is not "did the user like it" but **"did the machine run."** Runway is the clearest: *"Credits are only automatically returned when a generation ends in a generation error. If a generation completes — even if the result doesn't match your prompt… credits are consumed… Credits power the generation process itself, not the quality of the output."* (VERIFIED.) HeyGen states the rationale for never refunding: *"content creation on the platform utilizes resources that incur costs, regardless of whether the app met your expectations."* Nobody refunds a completed-but-disliked output. Three sidesteps are more loved than any refund: **free retakes** (ElevenLabs: two free regenerations, same prompt+voice+model, <2h, same page session), **graceful downgrade** (Suno drops exhausted paid users to the free 50/day; Ideogram drops them into the unlimited slow queue), and **goodwill grants that behave differently** (Suno: outage credits *"unlike monthly credits, these will carry over"*).

**Free grants.** Universally either *recurring-and-small* or *one-time-and-tiny*. Nobody grants a large lifetime allotment of the flagship artifact.

### 3c. The literature's case against credits — and why most of it misses Skipper

The strongest theoretical statement is Dholakia's **double conversion problem**: dollars→credits is posted and stable; credits→work is variable, vendor-controlled and opaque (VERIFIED, https://thepricingconundrum.substack.com/p/the-double-conversion-problem-in, 2026-07-18). Every documented pathology is a failure of the *second* conversion — Canva's agent burning ~100× a basic tool, HeyGen's 7× model gap, monday.com's four schedules inside one pool, Clay charging for failed enrichments (20–30% of allowance wasted, changed March 2026), Figma's live 20-vs-5-to-25 spread with staff answering that cost depends on *"type of AI action, complexity… the model used, and any context provided."* Real dated user testimony: *"Yesterday I payed 60 USD and they finished in about 3 or 4 prompts"* (AdiGrosman, 2026-03-27, https://forum.figma.com/report-a-problem-6/ai-credits-exhausted-despite-limited-usage-52254).

**Skipper has no second conversion.** 1 credit = 1 drive, deterministically. That immunity is the single most important fact in this memo, and the only way to lose it is to introduce the abstraction on purpose.

Two other findings that *do* transfer:
- **Redenomination reads as confiscation, not repricing.** Cursor moved Pro from "500 fast requests" to "$20 of frontier model usage," users burned it in a handful of prompts, and the CEO apologized and refunded three weeks of charges (VERIFIED, https://cursor.com/blog/june-2025-pricing, 2025-07-04: *"We were not clear that 'unlimited usage' was only for Auto and not all other models."*). ClassPass's 2018 class-count→variable-credit switch produced *"A 9 credit class is 20 credits over the weekend? Is this a glitch or are you actually trying to rob me?"* (VERIFIED, Refinery29, 2018-03). Nothing structural was reverted in either case; the damage was to trust. **Implication: "1 credit = 1 drive" must be a permanent public promise. If drives ever differentiate, sell a different SKU, never a different rate.**
- **The bundle literature says a big prepaid grant maximizes non-consumption.** Soman & Gourville's transaction decoupling (JMR 38(1), 2001) and Gourville & Soman's payment depreciation (JCR 25(2), 1998) — theater season-ticket no-show rates rise monotonically with bundle size, 0.6% (1 play) → 21.5% (4) (REPORTED, via Dholakia, Psychology Today 2016-11-14; primary PDFs 403'd). A 100-unit lifetime grant is the largest-bundle, longest-horizon configuration in that entire literature. It doesn't just fail to monetize; it plausibly *suppresses use*.
- **Showing the meter suppresses use too.** A 2026 teardown of Gamma (constant balance + per-action cost) names the consequence: *"Reduced experimentation due to visible resource depletion"* (REPORTED, https://www.data-mania.com/blog/pricing-transparency/, 2026-07-28). Hiding it causes bill shock (Perplexity, Cursor); showing it causes hoarding. **The resolution is a threshold: the meter appears only when it is about to bind.**

Distribution, for calibration: **~13%** of 800 AI agent companies use per-credit as the primary metric (REPORTED, Ibbaka, 2025-09-06); the widely-cited "126% surge in credit pricing" was **35 → 79 companies** against a 30,000+ SaaS market, i.e. <1% (REPORTED, https://softwarepricing.com/blog/credit-based-pricing-ai/). Credits are loud, not dominant. ⚠ Do not cite the "Bessemer: hybrid 27%→41%" figure — I fetched Bessemer's actual Feb 2026 playbook and it contains no market-distribution percentages and discloses no methodology (VERIFIED by absence, https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-ai-pricing-and-monetization-playbook).

## 4. The inversion problem

**The shape.** `POST /drives/plan` spends Opus tokens on every turn. `POST /drives/propose` spends a Google Routes call. Both are designed to fire anonymously, forever, with no credit attached. `POST /drives` — the *only* thing that costs a credit — reuses pre-generated audio and costs approximately nothing to serve. **The credit is a value instrument, not a cost-control instrument.** Any attempt to make it do the second job breaks the first.

### 4a. Correcting the cost model (two errors that were in the raw research)

⚠ **Google Routes: Skipper bills Essentials, not Pro.** `packages/routing/src/index.ts` sets `origin`, `destination`, `intermediates`, `travelMode: 'DRIVE'`, `polylineQuality: 'HIGH_QUALITY'` — **no `routingPreference`**, no `optimizeWaypointOrder`, no side-of-road/heading modifiers, and `via` caps intermediates at 8. Per Google's SKU trigger list (VERIFIED, https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/sku-details, fetched 2026-07-31), Pro requires 11–25 waypoints, `optimizeWaypointOrder: true`, a `TRAFFIC_AWARE*` preference, or side-of-road/heading/vehicle-stopover modifiers. None apply. So: **Compute Routes Essentials, $5.00 per 1,000 calls, 10,000 free/month** (https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing) — half the marginal price and double the free tier that the raw research assumed. **⚠ Live cost-regression landmine: "side of road" is a Pro trigger *and* is native Skipper vocabulary (break-stop pull-offs; `sideOfRoad` already exists in `packages/studio/src/eval/grounding.ts`). Adding a side-of-road modifier to a Routes waypoint silently doubles the SKU and halves the free tier with no error and no review signal.** That belongs in a code comment at the call site, not only here.

**Anthropic, dated 2026-07-31** (VERIFIED, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing.md): Opus 5 **$5/MTok in, $25/MTok out**; 5-minute cache write $6.25, 1-hour $10, **cache hits & refreshes $0.50**. Sonnet 5 **$2/$10 through 2026-08-31, then $3/$15**. Haiku 4.5 $1/$5. Batch API **50% off input and output**. ⚠ Two things not in the raw modelling: tool-use adds a **286-token** system overhead per request (`auto`/`none`) or **406** (`any`/`tool`); and Opus 4.7+ use a tokenizer producing **~30% more tokens for the same text**. Also: the 5-minute cache's *TTL extension* is free, but the refresh itself bills as a cache read at 0.1× — the column is literally named "Cache Hits & Refreshes."

**Worst-case exposure under the caps as they exist in `apps/api/src/limits.ts` today** (my arithmetic, from verified rates + the file's own constants):
- Per turn: input ≤ ~4,000 tok (12,000 chars ÷ ~3, the file's own pessimistic ratio) × $5/MTok = **$0.020**; output ≤ `PLANNER_MAX_TOKENS` (2,048) × $25/MTok = **$0.0512**. **≈ $0.071/turn worst case**, ~$0.053 warm-cached.
- `PLAN_RATE_HOUR` (120/hr) → 2,880 turns/day **per IP per Cloud Run instance** → **≈ $205/day/IP/instance**. 100 rotating residential IPs ≈ **$20,500/day**, same order of magnitude as the documented LLMjacking figure of ~$46,000/day (REPORTED, Sysdig, https://www.sysdig.com/blog/llmjacking-stolen-cloud-credentials-used-in-new-ai-attack).
- **72% of that worst case is OUTPUT.** `MAX_PLAN_TOTAL_CHARS` — which the file calls "the cap that maps to dollars" — governs the other 28%. **`PLANNER_MAX_TOKENS` is the actual wallet guard**, and its comment says "raising it costs nothing unless the model actually generates the tokens." True for a rider; false for an adversary pinned at the ceiling. That comment deserves a second sentence.
- Attacker economics: residential proxies run ~$1–$8/GB (REPORTED, vendor pricing pages — inherently marketing sources). A ~5 KB turn means 1 GB ≈ 200,000 requests for ~$4. Per-IP limiting does not change that ratio; it only sets how many IPs get rented.

⚠ **Discrepancy to settle before trusting any anonymous cost model.** As of the tree today, `apps/api/src/drives.ts:377` is `driveRoutes.use('*', withSession, requireAccount)` — the whole `/drives` sub-app, **including `/propose`**, is behind a real account. `apps/api/src/index.ts:8` agrees: *"POST /drives/propose → preview the route for a picked A→B (free account; no credit)."* CLAUDE.md's invariant says the opposite (per-ROUTE, never on the mount, propose open to anonymous). **Today's code has no anonymous paid surface at all.** Either 1.1 hasn't opened it yet, or the invariant is describing intent the code hasn't reached. Whichever it is, the practical consequence is good news: **the cheapest moment to bound this is before the door opens, and that moment is now.**

### 4b. What others do about an expensive conversation

Ranked by structural closeness:

1. **Windsurf — ran it, killed it, published why** (§2, finding 1). The metered surface *was* the conversation; Tab autocomplete was unlimited. Replaced by quotas. This is the closest thing to a controlled experiment on Skipper's question.
2. **Claude Code and OpenAI Codex — the most expensive conversations in the category, neither metered per turn.** Both use rolling 5-hour windows plus weekly caps. Codex grants mid-turn grace (*"If you reach your usage limit during an active turn, the agent will be able to continue working on that turn"*); Claude Code requires **explicit consent** before any API-rate overflow bills. VERIFIED (learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing; support.claude.com).
3. **GitHub Copilot — the same two-surface split, opposite polarity.** Completions are *"included and don't consume credits"* forever; discrete agentic requests consume AI Credits (VERIFIED, github.blog, credits replaced premium requests 2026-06-01). It works because Copilot's free surface is also its *cheap* surface. Skipper's free surface is its *expensive* one — Copilot gives no guidance for the inverted case.
4. **Replit — the explicit statement of Skipper's problem, unshipped.** Post-backlash it committed to *"distinguishing dialogue intent from code modification intent."* A year later the live policy is still *"All Agent interactions are billable."* That the easy-sounding fix hasn't shipped is itself a finding.
5. **DuckDuckGo's Duck.ai — the only product matching Skipper's shape (anonymous, no account, operator eats the API bill), and it solves it by not putting a frontier model on the free tier at all.** Anonymous gets Claude 4.5 Haiku / Mistral Small / GPT-5.4 nano-mini class; Claude Opus 4.8 sits behind $19.99/mo. The daily anonymous limit exists and is **deliberately unpublished**: *"we cannot divulge the exact method in order to protect our system from bad actors… it is engineered in such a way that makes it completely anonymous"* (VERIFIED, https://duckduckgo.com/duckduckgo-help-pages/duckai/usage-limits).
6. **Autio — the travel-category near-miss.** Its content is a shared corpus assembled along whatever route you drive, structurally identical to Skipper. It *names* a SKU after the rider's action — "Single Trip" — and implements it as a **30-day window, $15.99**. Even a corpus-assembly product could not find a way to bill per-drive. Its free tier meters **content units (5 stories)**, not time.

**The hole nobody could fill:** in every metered case, planning is billed *in addition to* a billed artifact — never *instead of* a free one. **No product charges for the conversation and gives the artifact away.** Skipper's true mirror image has no shipped example to copy. That is a genuine gap, and it is an argument against inventing one.

### 4c. The honest options for Skipper's inversion

**(i) Meter the conversation.** Attach a fractional credit or a turn price to `/drives/plan`. *For:* aligns charge with cost; Lovable proves it's shippable. *Against:* it is exactly what Windsurf killed, for exactly the reason that binds here — the planner conversation *is* the charm, and a priced turn makes riders terse. It also introduces the second conversion Skipper currently doesn't have, and it prices the one surface with no eval gate in front of it. **Recommend against, strongly.**

**(ii) Gate the conversation behind a free account.** *For:* gives a stable metering key that survives IP rotation; costs an attacker one extra automated request. *Against:* ⚠ Apple 5.1.1(i) — *"Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality… If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login"* (VERIFIED). Skipper *does* have significant account features (owned drives, the ledger), so walling `POST /drives` is squarely fine — but the planner mints **no** account-bound artifact, so requiring signup merely to *talk* is precisely where 5.1.1(i) bites. This is the first rules-based argument for the existing invariant (planner open, wall at `POST /drives`), rather than a product one. **Use the session as a metering key, never as a gate.**

**(iii) Bound it with capacity, not price** — the category consensus. Concretely, in rough cost-effectiveness order:
  - **Prompt-cache the planner prefix and assert the hit.** ~75% off the input bill; free to implement. Keep persona + tool schema + anchor allowlist byte-stable ahead of the breakpoint. ⚠ A cache miss is **silent** — `cache_read_input_tokens` comes back 0 with no error — so a single stray `Date.now()` in the prefix costs 10× invisibly. Assert `cache_read_input_tokens > 0` in a test or a startup log. ⚠ This freezes the front half of a prompt CLAUDE.md wants iterated on, and a model swap invalidates it entirely.
  - **Per-conversation token budget, not a request count.** History grows quadratically without caching, so a *request* cap does not cap dollars. ⚠ An in-memory budget inherits the per-instance flaw `rate-limit.ts` already documents. Making the budget travel in a signed, server-issued token the client re-sends is self-limiting: dropping it starts a fresh conversation with no history.
  - **A global anonymous-tier daily token ceiling with a circuit breaker + push alert.** One counter, one env var. This is the control that converts "unbounded" into "bounded," which is the only property that ultimately matters. ⚠ Trip **only the anonymous tier** — otherwise you've handed an attacker a denial-of-service for the price of a denial-of-wallet.
  - **Degrade in persona, never with a 429.** Windsurf routes to free in-house models; Codex finishes the turn. Skipper already owns the right behaviour: the in-persona deflection. A rider who hits the ceiling should meet a Skipper who's cagey, not an HTTP error. This is the one place where optimizing for charm and optimizing for cost point the same way.
  - **Model choice at conversation *start*, not mid-flight.** Sonnet 5 cuts the per-turn ceiling ~2.5×, Haiku 4.5 ~5×. ⚠ But the planner is the first time the persona speaks ungated, and a mid-conversation swap is a voice change the rider *hears*, on the one surface where charm is the product. It also invalidates the cache (caches are model-scoped). If you do it, decide at turn 0 by tier.
  - **App Attest** binds a request to scarce *hardware* rather than a rotatable network property — the only control here whose bypass cost scales with devices. ⚠ Days of real crypto work (x5c chain, CBOR, OID `1.2.840.113635.100.8.2`, a persisted monotonic counter, a public-key-rebinding guard), plus a second integration for the receipt risk metric; keys die on reinstall; it stops scripts, not device farms; and the dev-bypass path you build becomes the attack surface (`ADMIN_DEV_BYPASS=1` already shows how those drift). **Build it when anonymous spend is measured and material — the cheap controls above bound the loss in an afternoon.**

**(iv) A 50% operator-side lever nobody costed.** All the rider-side analysis missed that the **Batch API is 50% off input and output** and fits the paid *studio* pipeline exactly — `generate-narrations`, `enrich-pois`, `classify-treatments` are non-latency-sensitive, bounded, and already `--apply`-gated. Opus 5 batch = $2.50/$12.50. Historic corpus spend is where the real dollars have actually gone. Explicitly **not** applicable to `/drives/plan` (stateful, interactive). This is a cost win that requires no pricing decision at all.

## 5. Apple's rules as a hard constraint (verified current 2026-07-31)

**You must sell credits via IAP.** 3.1.1: *"If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app… you must use in-app purchase. Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality."* All six 3.1.3 exceptions fail: reader apps (no external catalog), multiplatform (permits *honoring* web purchases only if the same item is also sold as IAP — moot, no web surface), enterprise, person-to-person (the persona is a model), goods consumed outside the app (the audio is played *by* the app), free companion to a paid web tool.

**A credit pack is a consumable.** Apple: *"A product that is used once, after which it becomes depleted and must be purchased again."* Consumables aren't restorable through StoreKit's restore flow, so the "restore mechanism" 3.1.1 asks for **is** the server-side ledger keyed to a real account. Skipper already has it.

**Purchased credits may never expire** (§2, finding 5). Free grants are outside that text.

**Commission — from the contract, not a marketing page.** Schedule 2 to the Developer Program License Agreement, **v126, 17 December 2025** (VERIFIED, https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/schedules/Schedule-2-and-3-English.pdf), §3.4(a) 30%; §3.4(b) **App Store Small Business Program 15%** for developers with *"no more than $1,000,000 in total proceeds… during the twelve (12) fiscal months occurring in the prior calendar year."* Unchanged in 2025–26. Skipper qualifies. The reduced rate takes effect **15 days after the end of the fiscal month in which enrollment is approved** — enroll before the first sale, not after.

**⚠ US external purchase links: current state, and it is unsettled.** Verbatim from the guidelines today: 3.1.1(a) — *"These entitlements are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps"*; and the prohibition applies *"In all other storefronts, except for the United States storefront, where this prohibition does not apply."* The same carve-out is now in the **binding contract**, Schedule 2 v126 §3.11. Timeline: 2025-04-30 N.D. Cal. holds Apple in contempt and enjoins the 27% link-out fee; **2025-12-11 the Ninth Circuit affirms contempt but narrows the remedy** — Apple may charge *"a commission based on costs that are genuinely and reasonably necessary for its coordination of external links,"* **once the district court approves a rate**, and may require that external links not be *more prominent* than Apple's; 2026-05-06 SCOTUS denies Apple's stay application; **2026-06-30 SCOTUS grants cert, No. 25-1311, limited to Question 1** (whether civil contempt can rest on an injunction's "spirit"), with Apple's brief due **2026-09-14** after a 2026-07-23 extension, Epic's **2026-11-13**, argument unscheduled (VERIFIED, https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/apple-inc-v-epic-games-inc-2/, fetched today). Apple moved on 2026-07-07 to stay the remand rate-setting; **UNCERTAIN whether the district court has ruled** — I found no reporting either way.
**Practical reading: the US external-link commission is 0% today, could become nonzero on short notice, and the carve-out is US-storefront only.** Secondary sources still saying "Apple charges 27%" are describing the pre-April-2025 structure and are wrong on tense. **Do not ship a link-out.** If you ever do: gate on `Storefront`, not locale; keep the IAP alongside it (the injunction removed anti-steering, not the 3.1.1 IAP requirement); don't make the link more prominent than the IAP button.

**Refunds — you have no authority and no veto.** Schedule 2 §6.3: Apple *"may refund to the End-User the full amount of the price paid… You shall reimburse, or grant Apple a credit for, an amount equal to the price."* Full price, not net of commission. The wire protocol you'd owe: `ONE_TIME_CHARGE` → grant; `CONSUMPTION_REQUEST` → **respond within 12 hours** via `Send Consumption Information`, and only if `customerConsented` is true; `REFUND` → clawback; `REFUND_DECLINED`; **`REFUND_REVERSED` → *"If your app revoked content or services as a result of the related refund, it needs to reinstate them"*** (all VERIFIED from Apple's own documentation). Non-response to a consumption request effectively concedes the refund (REPORTED, RevenueCat/Qonversion).
**⚠ `sampleContentProvided: true` is a real asset for Skipper** — `GET /sample`, the anonymous planner conversation, and the one free preview clip from the rider's own route are exactly the free trial that field asks about. 1.1's ungated front door is, incidentally, refund-abuse armor.

**Metadata rules with teeth.** 2.3.1(a): *"promoting a false price, whether within or outside of the App Store, is grounds for removal… and termination of your developer account."* Render `Product.displayPrice`; never hardcode a price string. 2.3.2 requires the App Store description to disclose that items require additional purchase; 2.3.7 keeps prices out of the name/subtitle/screenshots. There is **no** Apple rule against showing a credit balance in-app (VERIFIED by absence).

**Outside the US, briefly.** EU DMA alternative terms are 10%/17% + 3% for App Store payment processing, with a Core Technology Commission replacing the per-install fee — ⚠ **UNCERTAIN whether the promised consolidation to a single business model actually completed**; one secondary source says as of mid-2026 it hasn't. Brazil: from **iOS 26.5** (announced 2026-06-18) alternative distribution and payments are permitted under a revised Attachment 12. **For a toy/lifestyle project the correct posture is: IAP everywhere, no link-out, revisit never.**

**Unread, and flagged:** the 2026-06-08 release note says the DPLA updated **Attachment 2, §1.1 (In-App Purchase API)**. Nobody read it. Do so before building purchase plumbing.

## 6. What Skipper already has right

This section exists because being told "you already did the hard part" is worth as much as a recommendation. **Do not rebuild the ledger.** Specifically, the research validates:

- **Append-only ledger, balance = SUM(amount), immutable rows, compensating entries.** This is exactly the structure Apple's refund flow requires (a `REFUND` must be a compensating entry, never a mutation — the original grant is the evidentiary record behind both a deferred-revenue balance and an Apple transaction) and exactly what ASC 606 contract-liability accounting wants. Runway's "the spend row disappears from the usage table" is the same pattern reached independently.
- **UNIQUE `idempotency_key` per movement.** Apple redelivers notifications on any non-2xx. A global unique key on `apple_iap:<transactionId>` makes redelivery a no-op *and* defeats receipt-replay across accounts. The mechanism you already built is the mechanism the IAP integration needs.
- **1 credit = 1 drive.** This is the *good* kind of credit — a natural unit of value, no exchange rate, no matrix, no second conversion. Nearly every documented credit failure (Figma 5-vs-25, monday.com's four schedules, HeyGen's 7×, Canva's 100×, ClassPass) is a failure of the abstracted-bundle design. **The literature's case against credits is largely a case against a different design than yours.**
- **Lifetime, never expires.** Required by Apple 3.1.1 for purchased credits, marketed as the headline benefit by every surviving competitor, and a condition many states attach to their gift-card escheat exemptions. Say it out loud in paywall copy — it's a free trust asset in a category where expiry is the norm.
- **Never refunded on delete.** Category-normal (Shaka Guide, GuideAlong, Action Tour Guide all all-sales-final with narrow discretionary windows) and consistent with Runway's principle: *"Credits power the generation process itself, not the quality of the output."* One refinement worth considering: encode the rule as *"a credit is consumed when a drive becomes playable,"* not *"when a row is inserted"* — a drive that can't resolve every stop to audio should never have cost anything.
- **The wall at `POST /drives`, planner open.** Now supported by an Apple rules argument (5.1.1(i)), not just product taste.
- **Rider-facing copy already says "drives," not "credits."** `apps/mobile/app/index.tsx:203-205` renders `N free drive(s) left`, and the comment at line 200 already names the anti-pattern ("informational, not a depleting 'X-left-of-N' toll gauge"). That call is correct and this research confirms it. Two follow-ups: treat "credit" as banned in rider-facing strings (admin copy is fine), and **reject the "N of 100" framing** that `creditSummary`'s docstring floats — that's precisely the depleting gauge shown to suppress use.
- **The rate limiter is not a stopgap ahead of a real model — it *is* the model the category converged on.** Windsurf, Claude Code and Codex all landed on time-boxed quotas for expensive conversations. `apps/api/src/limits.ts` — one file, argued numbers, written *before* the route that consumes them — is a genuinely better artifact than most vendors have. Its one real weakness is that the buckets are per-instance and in-memory.
- **The credit ledger is separate from the cost controls.** Keeping those two instruments distinct is the single decision that prevents Skipper from walking into the Windsurf failure.

## 7. Options, with a recommendation

### Option A — "Measurement window": keep everything, sell nothing, bound the wallet
**Mechanics.** Ledger unchanged. Free grant stays large. No IAP. Ship the global anonymous token ceiling + circuit breaker, the prompt-cache assertion, the persona-shaped cap message, and the `PLANNER_MAX_TOKENS` comment fix. Write into `docs/decisions/credit-ledger.md` that the grant is a dated measurement window and that the launch cohort will never buy a pack.
**Build cost.** Days, mostly in `limits.ts` and one counter.
**Risk.** Zero revenue, forever, for everyone created under the current grant. The 100-value is a *dead zone* — too large to monetize, too large to ration, but small enough to render a counter, so it pays the costs of a meter for a gate that never fires.
**Right for:** a founder who is certain the answer is "this is a toy, it never charges." That is a legitimate answer for this project and should not be argued out of.

### Option B — Drive packs: small lifetime grant + never-expiring consumables
**Mechanics.** Cut `FREE_DRIVE_CAP` to a small number **now, before a real cohort exists**. Sell consumables named in *drives* — a ladder anchored to the category's revealed band, e.g. 1 drive $4.99 / 3 for $9.99 / 10 for $24.99 (all below the $14.99–$19.99 per-*tour* norm, because a Skipper drive is one route, not a park's whole catalog). Comps and dogfooding become large admin grants — the ledger already supports that, and CLAUDE.md already says a comp *is* a large grant. Purchase behind `requireAccount`. Cost stated on the confirm CTA **only below a low-balance threshold** (~5), hint hidden above it.
**Build cost.** The ledger, `db.batch` consume and idempotency machinery are **unchanged**. New work is the IAP surface: consumable SKUs in App Store Connect, an App Store Server Notifications V2 endpoint with JWS verification, a client-initiated verify path (webhooks are at-least-once *and* at-most-never), provider transaction columns on `credit_entries`, `appAccountToken` plumbing (⚠ must be a client-persisted UUID, **not** the user id — the anonymous row is hard-deleted at link), the 12-hour `CONSUMPTION_REQUEST` responder, purchased-vs-free lot separation, negative-balance UX, and a Small Business Program enrollment. Call it 2–3 focused weeks with review cycles, plus a founder decision on `purgeUserData` vs. retained financial records.
**Risk.** The rider's imported mental model is *"$16.99 buys me Yellowstone forever."* A per-drive charge on the *same route* reads as worse than Shaka Guide **unless re-driving a saved drive is explicitly free** — it already is (content resolves live by subject id). Make that the headline, not a footnote.
**Right for:** wanting real revenue signal without changing the architecture.

### Option C — Per-region one-time unlock, $14.99–$19.99
**Mechanics.** Buy a region, unlimited drives in it, forever. Charges for the thing that's expensive to *make* (a region's corpus — the operator pipeline spend) rather than the thing that's cheap to *serve*.
**Build cost.** ⚠ High and architectural. **Region is deliberately a BBOX, never a stored FK** (`docs/decisions/geometry-first-regions.md`); a drive derives region by intersect. Making a region a purchasable entity re-introduces exactly the FK the architecture removed on purpose, and needs an entitlement check on every drive. There is also **one released region today**, so a "region unlock" is functionally "unlock the app."
**Risk.** Contradicts a settled architectural decision to chase a revenue model the founder may never want.
**Right for:** a future where 5+ regions exist and riders self-select geographically.

### Option D — Annual all-access, $19.99–$35.99, or hybrid unlock + all-access
**Mechanics.** Autio $35.99/yr, Action+ $99.99/yr, Roadtrippers $35.99/yr all sit here; RevenueCat puts travel's **median annual plan at $20 — the lowest of any category** (VERIFIED, https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-travel/, published 2026-03-19, 115,000+ apps). **66% of travel revenue is annual plans**; monthly is structurally dead for a 2–6-trips-a-year rider.
**Risk.** With one region, an annual sub is a visibly worse deal than a $16.99 one-time purchase and riders will see that. Also: travel's top decile makes ~$822/mo one year post-launch, median ~$35/mo. That ceiling is small, and it is the ceiling the anonymous planner spend has to be defensible against.
**Right for:** later, with breadth.

### RECOMMENDATION

**Option B, sequenced: decide the free grant now; build the purchase plumbing later or never.**

The defence has three parts.

**First, the only irreversible decision is the grant number, and it is irreversible *per user*.** Everything else in this memo — pack prices, IAP webhooks, region unlocks, expiry policy — can be decided in 2027 at the same cost as today. The grant cannot: `freeGrantEntry` freezes `FREE_DRIVE_CAP` into the row, and the ledger is immutable by design. Every account minted at today's value carries it forever. Right now there are effectively no real accounts. **This is the cheapest this decision will ever be, and it gets monotonically more expensive with every signup.** Concretely: cut it to **3–5**, and hand out comps as large admin grants — which is what the ledger was built for, and which lets TestFlight riders and friends stay unlimited without freezing "unlimited" into the general cohort.

**Second, Option B is the only shape that requires changing nothing you've already decided well.** It keeps the artifact-denominated unit (immunity to the entire double-conversion literature), the lifetime/no-expiry promise (Apple-required, category-marketed, escheat-friendly), the drives-not-credits vocabulary, the geometry-first region model, and the separation between the ledger and the wallet caps. Options C and D each require unwinding a settled architectural or product decision to chase revenue on a product that may never launch. That is the wrong trade for a toy/lifestyle project.

**Third, it correctly refuses to fix the inversion.** The conversation stays free, unmetered, and bounded by capacity rather than price — the one move Windsurf reversed in public, Replit couldn't ship, and only Lovable still runs. The credit keeps doing the job it is good at: pricing a discrete, ownable artifact the rider *wants*, at the emotional peak of the conversation, with no arithmetic in the way.

**What Option B does *not* license:** building the IAP stack now. Nothing sells today, 1.0 may never be released, and the whole purchase surface is deferrable at no cost. The recommendation is *decide the grant, keep the ledger, harden the wallet, write the pack ladder down, and stop.*

## 8. Open questions for the founder

Ordered by which must be answered first. Each is a decision with options, not homework.

**Q1 — The free grant. This is the only irreversible one; every day of delay costs a cohort.**
 (a) **Cut to 3–5 now**, comps as admin grants — *recommended*. Downside: if a friend signs up before you comp them, they hit a wall you have to fix by hand.
 (b) Keep the current value as an explicitly dated measurement window, written into `docs/decisions/credit-ledger.md`, accepting in writing that the launch cohort never converts. Downside: pays the cost of a meter for a gate that never fires.
 (c) Grant effectively-infinite (e.g. 10,000) and render **no counter at all** — the honest "this is free" position. Downside: forecloses charging that cohort, but at least stops teaching riders that drives are rationed.
 ⚠ (b) is the worst of the three; (a) and (c) are both coherent.

**Q2 — Does the anonymous planner actually ship open?** The code today wa lls all of `/drives*` including `/propose` at the mount (`drives.ts:377`), and `index.ts:8` documents propose as "free account." CLAUDE.md says the opposite. (a) Open propose per-route as designed and accept anonymous Routes spend at Essentials rates ($5/1k, 10k free/mo). (b) Keep it account-gated and let the anonymous surface be plan-only. **Until this is settled, no anonymous cost model is trustworthy.** Five minutes of reading, not a research question.

**Q3 — A global (cross-instance) anonymous wallet ceiling: build it before the planner ships, or after?** (a) **Before** — one counter, one env var, one push alert; converts an unbounded liability into a known one, and the worst-case arithmetic (~$205/day per IP per instance) says the liability is real. (b) After, once spend is measured. Downside of (b): the measurement *is* the bill.

**Q4 — If/when you sell, which shape?** (a) Drive packs (Option B). (b) Region unlock (Option C) — only if regions multiply. (c) Annual all-access (Option D) — only with breadth. Not urgent; nothing sells today.

**Q5 — Should the *free* grant expire?** Legally and store-rule clean (3.1.1 covers only purchased credits), and the `expires_at` column already exists reserved. (a) No — simplest, and it protects the "never expires" story you'd otherwise have to caveat. (b) Yes — the only credit-side lever against a large grant, but it requires FIFO/expiring-first consumption logic `creditSummary()` doesn't have, and expiry on anything reliably reads as extraction. **Only worth revisiting if Q1 resolves to (b) or (c).**

**Q6 — Post-refund policy, and whether `purgeUserData` may retain a financial record.** Only decidable once something sells, but it has a schema consequence, so it's cheaper to decide before the first sale: a refund of already-spent credits drives the balance negative (correct — it's a debt), while `drives.ts` currently shows `Math.max(0, remaining)`, so the rider sees an unexplained "0". And `purgeUserData` deletes the whole ledger, which after money is involved destroys the transaction→user mapping needed to honor a post-deletion `REFUND`. Options: (a) retain a pseudonymized financial record (transaction id, amount, dates, no PII) under a legal-obligation carve-out while still purging drives and identity; (b) accept that post-deletion refunds are unanswerable. **App Store 5.1.1(v) requires account deletion; it does not require destroying tax records.**

**Two things worth doing regardless of every answer above**, because they're free and they're cost regressions waiting to happen: (1) a comment at the Routes call site naming **side-of-road / heading / `TRAFFIC_AWARE` / >10 waypoints / `optimizeWaypointOrder` as SKU-escalation triggers** — the Pro SKU doubles the price and halves the free tier silently; (2) a second sentence on `PLANNER_MAX_TOKENS` noting that "raising it costs nothing" is true for a rider and false for an adversary pinned at the ceiling, since **72% of worst-case planner spend is output tokens**.
